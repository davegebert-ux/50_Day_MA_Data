
MA RESPECT REDESIGN - CONCEPTUAL NOTES (session in progress, not yet coded)
============================================================
OLD (broken) definition: for each of 4 MAs (10/20/50/100), count violations
(closes below), depth, recovery time over a 60-day window; take best-of-4
bucket score. Problem: almost any stock respects at least one of four MAs
well enough to max out - 93%+ of the large 456-event sample scored a
perfect 5/5. Zero discriminating power.

FIRST REDESIGN ATTEMPT (rejected as too crude): % of days price closed
at/above each MA over a lookback window, various blends tested (simple
average, max-of-3, weighted toward 50MA, "relevance"-gated). Best variant
(max-of-3, continuous) only reached ~0.09 correlation - better than the old
broken version but still weak, and Dave correctly flagged that this doesn't
actually measure what he cares about.

WHAT DAVE ACTUALLY WANTS THIS ATTRIBUTE TO MEASURE (clarified this session):
Not "was price above or below the line" (too binary/crude). Real target
concept has three intertwined pieces:

1. TOUCH QUALITY - when price does approach/test a moving average, is it a
   gentle touch-and-bounce (shallow penetration, quick recovery) or a hard
   break-and-linger (deep penetration, slow/no recovery)? This is closer to
   the OLD depth/recovery-day logic, but needs to apply per-MA rather than
   picking whichever MA looks best.

2. MA ORDER / POSTURE (specifically 10 vs 20) - are the 10-day and 20-day
   MAs staying cleanly stacked and running roughly parallel (orderly trend
   signature), or crossing back and forth over each other repeatedly
   (choppiness/indecision signature)? This is a genuinely new dimension not
   captured by either the old or first-redesign versions - it's about the
   MAs' relationship to EACH OTHER, not just price's relationship to a
   single MA.

3. ESCALATION / WHICH LINE ACTUALLY GETS DEFENDED - different stocks find
   support at different MAs depending on their character (fast movers may
   only ever need the 10; slower/more mature movers may test all the way to
   the 50). The real underlying question isn't "did it respect the 10, the
   20, or the 50" as separate checks - it's: IS THERE EVIDENCE OF A
   CONSISTENTLY DEFENDED FLOOR AT ALL, wherever that floor happens to sit
   for this particular stock? I.e., does this look like institutions are
   actively defending some level on pullbacks, repeatedly, with real
   evidence in the price history - vs. no discernible defended level
   anywhere (random/choppy price action with no consistent floor)?

Framing Dave used: "if we can't see [institutional defense] in history,
there's not a good reason to believe they're going to when we actually try
and take a trade." So the attribute should reward CONSISTENCY OF DEFENSE
(same MA, or a graduated set of MAs, repeatedly holding on pullbacks) over
any specific MA period being the "right" one.

100-day MA dropped from consideration per Dave (not actually traded off of).
Recency matters - roughly a 1-month (~20-22 trading day) lookback window
discussed as more relevant than very short (10-day) or the old 60-day
window, though this may need revisiting once the new logic is built.

NEXT STEP (not yet done): translate this three-part concept into actual
mechanical scoring logic - likely need: (a) a per-MA touch-quality measure
(depth + recovery, similar to old logic but applied individually, not
best-of), (b) a 10/20 crossover-frequency measure (count of crosses in
lookback window, or % time spent in "stacked" order), and (c) some way of
identifying which MA (if any) is acting as the "defended floor" for this
specific stock and rewarding consistency of defense at that level, not just
its identity. Not yet built or tested against the large sample.


FOLLOW-UP - RESOLVED: HOW TO HANDLE "WHICH MA IS THE DEFENDED FLOOR" (piece 3)
============================================================
Dave's resolution: don't try to algorithmically pick which single MA (10,
20, or 50) is "the" defended one - instead, measure genuine touch quality
independently for each of the three, then take the BEST of the three
(similar structural pattern to both the old broken version and the first
redesign attempt's best-performing variant). Preference/priority naturally
given to the 50 if it's respected, since that's the actual trade trigger,
but a stock respecting only its 10 (fast mover) or only its 20 (medium mover)
should still score well - the specific MA identity isn't the point, evidence
of SOME consistently defended level is the point.

KEY DIFFERENCE FROM THE OLD BROKEN VERSION (why this shouldn't just re-max
everyone out again): the old version broke because "best of four" was
layered on top of a loose, easily-satisfied violation-bucket test (basically
any recovery within a generous window scored well). This redesign instead
requires each individual MA's touch-quality sub-score to be a genuinely
strict, continuous measure of depth and recovery speed FIRST, and only takes
best-of-3 across that stricter measure. A stock can "win" on its best MA and
still score low if even its best touch was sloppy (deep penetration, slow
recovery) - so best-of-3 no longer guarantees an easy max score the way
best-of-4 did on the old loose test.

Practical structure to build next: 
  1. Per-MA touch-quality sub-score (10, 20, 50 - continuous, not bucketed
     pass/fail) based on penetration depth and recovery speed, over the
     ~1-month lookback window
  2. Take best-of-3 as one component of the final MA Respect score
  3. Separately, still incorporate the 10/20 crossover-frequency /
     parallel-stacking measure (piece 2 from earlier notes) as a second
     component - this captures trend orderliness distinctly from touch
     quality and hasn't been resolved/discussed yet in terms of how to
     combine the two
NOT YET DONE: actually coding piece 1 (strict per-MA touch quality) and
piece 2 (crossover frequency), then deciding how to combine them into one
final score, then testing against the 456-event sample.


FOLLOW-UP - RESOLVED: CROSSOVER MEASURE DEFINITION (piece 2)
============================================================
Considered two options: (a) simple count of MA10/MA20 crossover events
within the lookback window, or (b) a distance/separation-based measure
(how much daylight typically exists between the two lines, rewarding wide
consistent separation not just technical non-crossing).

Dave's decision: go with (a), simple crossover count. Reasoning: frequent
crossing between the 10 and 20 directly reflects short-term price
volatility/whipsaw, which is mechanically the thing most likely to trigger
stop-outs - so it's not just a proxy for "orderliness" in the abstract, it's
fairly directly tied to the actual risk the strategy cares about. Also
simpler to implement than a distance-based measure - Dave explicitly noted
ease of build as a factor.

Practical structure: count number of times MA10 crosses MA20 (in either
direction) within the ~1-month lookback window. Fewer crosses = better
(more orderly, less choppy) = higher score. Exact scoring thresholds (how
many crosses = what score) not yet defined - needs calibration against the
456-event sample once built.

STILL NOT YET RESOLVED: how to combine this crossover-count component with
the best-of-3 touch-quality component (piece 1) into one final MA Respect
score - e.g. simple sum, weighted average, or some other blend. Not yet
discussed.


FOLLOW-UP - RESOLVED: CROSSOVER COUNT DROPPED, TOUCH QUALITY IS THE FULL SCORE

Standalone comparison confirmed touch quality (corr 0.318/0.351) is vastly

ATTEMPT 7 - height consistency, baseline computed from WITHIN the window

candles DO relative to each other (CORT's candles mostly compound in the

confirms the problem is fundamentally about DIRECTIONAL PERSISTENCE of
candle-to-candle movement, not raw candle magnitude.

to the bottom score, no separation achieved at all.

=== WHIPSAW/CANDLE-CHARACTER: SOLVED - ATR VARIABILITY (attempt 10) ===

DATE: Resumed session following Dave's side conversation in a separate
thread where he researched industry-standard smoothness measures. That
conversation surfaced four candidates: ADX, R-squared of linear
regression, ATR variability, and Kaufman's Efficiency Ratio. ADX and
Efficiency Ratio were already tested/excluded in prior work. R-squared
and ATR variability were the two new ones to try.

R-SQUARED ATTEMPT: Tested 40-day lookback first - got clean CORT vs CXW
separation (0.819 vs 0.475) but THC scored badly (0.382) due to its
smooth rally-then-pullback shape: a straight regression line can't fit
a smooth curve, so R-squared conflates "orderly arc" with "random scatter."
Tried 20-day lookback to fix THC - CORT/UTI separated from CXW cleanly
but AMRX cratered to near-zero (0.016) despite being a confirmed fine
trade. Root cause: R-squared is fundamentally measuring "how well does
a straight line fit" - it cannot distinguish an orderly curve from chaos.
Structural limitation, not a tuning issue. Excluded.

ATR VARIABILITY - PROPERLY TUNED THIS TIME:
Dave correctly pushed back on the earlier ATR variability dismissal -
it had been ruled out before proper band optimization. Re-tested across
a grid of lookback periods (15, 20, 30, 40 days) and ATR smoothing
periods (3, 5, 10, 14 days). Multiple combinations showed clean
separation of good (CORT, UTI) vs bad (CXW, AMKR) examples.

WINNER: lookback=20 days, ATR smoothing period=10 days.
Metric: coefficient of variation (CV = std/mean) of the 10-day rolling
ATR%, measured as ATR as a percentage of price, over the 20-day window
before entry. Lower CV = more consistent true range = more predictable

=== NEW SCREENING LAYER: OVERHEAD RESISTANCE / ALL-TIME-HIGH CHECK ===
(Part of the pre-watchlist momentum screen stage, NOT a scorecard attribute,
NOT part of the daily 50-day MA touch-detection step)

CONTEXT: Dave's real weekly process has two stages: (1) run the mechanical
momentum screen (market cap >$100M, TTM rev growth YoY >0%, 6-month price
performance 30-500%, primary listing, SMA100>SMA200, ADX(50) 20-40, avg
10-day volume >1M, price>50-day MA), then (2) do a MANUAL VISUAL PASS over
the results - looking for a smooth bottom-left-to-top-right chart, ideally
making new highs (not recovering from a large past drawdown into overhead
resistance), and not reliant on a single big gap to reach its level - before
adding a name to the weekly watchlist. Only watchlist names get scanned
daily for 50-day MA touches. The 456-event sample and scorecard skip step
(2) entirely, likely explaining some of the RS/scoring noise found earlier
this session (e.g. UNM) - Dave concluded some touch events in the sample
are from tickers his own visual screen would have rejected.

THIS CHECK formalizes ONE piece of the visual pass: the "not recovering
into overhead resistance" idea. (Smoothness/path-directness and gap-driven
moves are separate, still-to-be-formalized pieces - gap piece may already
be covered by the Trend Efficiency gap check, TBD.)

ITERATION HISTORY:
  v1 (rejected as too loose): % drop from old high threshold. Tested 50%,
  then tightened to 40%, then 25% - at each threshold, false passes kept
  surfacing (ANIP, RES, CNM at 40%; SSD at 25%) where the chart was
  visually still choppy/range-bound even though the raw drawdown number
  fell under the line.
  KEY EXAMPLE - SSD: chart shows a strong 2023 uptrend, then nearly TWO
  YEARS of wide sideways chop between roughly 150 and 210, with multiple
  failed breakout attempts, before a recent grind back near the old highs.
  The 6-month momentum screen happened to catch it mid-upswing within that
  same tired range, making it look like fresh momentum. Any single
  high-to-low drawdown percentage misses this because it only evaluates
  the two most extreme points, not the fact that the stock spent ~2 years
  failing to make real progress. Dave: "this is not a good momentum name...
  it just happens to be high enough in the band that it's passing the
  screen."

  v2 (FINAL, adopted): abandoned the percentage-drop framework entirely.
  New rule: within a 2-year lookback, find the highest CLOSE that occurred
  MORE than a 2-month grace period ago (grace period exists so a stock
  that was simply at a slightly higher price 3-6 weeks ago, i.e. normal
  healthy pullback into its current entry zone, isn't penalized). If
  today's close is AT OR BELOW that older high, EXCLUDE - full stop, no
  threshold, no minimum drawdown required. If today's close is ABOVE that
  older high (already reclaimed/broken out to a fresh 2-year high),
  PASS - regardless of how severe the prior drawdown was in between.

RATIONALE (Dave, verbatim intent): "if there's overhead resistance, I don't
think it's broken out yet... we wanna be trading things that are broken
out and have momentum." Any unresolved old high, no matter how close,
means there are still sellers positioned to get out even, so it doesn't
qualify as a genuine breakout regardless of the exact drawdown magnitude.

VALIDATION - 15-name sample (as of 2026-08-19), v2 rule:
  EXCLUDE: MAN, PPC, TRU, MZTI, FLR, KD, PK, ANIP, RES, EFC, SSD, CTRE, CNM
  PASS: CDP, FCF (both made a fresh 2-year-plus high within the last 2
  months, no unresolved old high above current price)
  Note: this is a stricter bar than v1 - only 2 of 15 test names now pass,
  down from 4 of 15 at the 25% threshold. Dave confirmed this stricter
  behavior is exactly the intent.

FINAL FORMULA:
  lookback = 2 years, grace_period = 2 months (~60 days)
  eligible_high = max(close) over [today - 2yr, today - 2mo]
  if no eligible history yet (ticker too new) -> pass
  if current_close <= eligible_high -> EXCLUDE
  if current_close > eligible_high -> PASS

COMBINED-SCORING ARCHITECTURE DECIDED - ADDITIVE (2026-08-22)
============================================================
Long-deferred question: additive (average/sum across attributes) vs. veto
(weakest-link/min across attributes) for combining the 3 validated v2
attributes (Trend Efficiency, MA Respect, Relative Strength) into one
total score. Tested empirically against the 456-event sample rather than
resolved philosophically:

  Scheme              Corr w/ 20ma_P_R   Top-third avg   Bottom-third avg   Spread
  Additive (mean)      +0.135             0.410           -0.064            0.474
  Veto (min)            +0.062             0.323            0.044            0.278
  Hybrid (0 if any=0)   +0.098             (same as additive at >=3 cutoff)

DECISION: ADDITIVE (simple average of the 3 validated attributes) wins
clearly on every measure tested - higher correlation, cleaner top/bottom
separation. Veto underperforms because with only 3 attributes, requiring
all three to be strong is an overly harsh bar - a single unlucky reading
in one dimension disqualifies an otherwise-great trade, compounding the
same "one real event tanks a good setup" issue already found inside Trend
Efficiency alone (CORT/CFFN/LXP pattern). Additive lets two strong scores
reasonably outweigh one weak one, which matches the data better.

CAVEAT (important, logged for future reference): this conclusion is based
on combining only the 3 currently-validated attributes. If more attributes
are added in a future phase - especially noisier/unreviewed ones - a pure
additive approach could quietly let a genuinely broken attribute drag down
good trades without anyone noticing, which is exactly the blind spot veto-
style logic was originally meant to prevent. REVISIT this decision if/when
the attribute set changes materially - don't assume additive is correct
forever just because it's correct for today's 3-attribute scorecard.

STATUS: combined scoring architecture is now settled (additive/mean of the
3 validated v2 attributes) for the current scorecard. Not yet implemented
as a formal function in scorecard.py - logged as the decision, next step
would be adding a simple score_total() or similar if/when Dave wants it
coded in.
